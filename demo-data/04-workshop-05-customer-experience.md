# Workshop 05 - Customer Experience and Accessibility

Date: 2 July 2026. Facilitator: Priya Raghavan. Transcribed automatically.

AISHA BELL (Customer Experience Lead, Meridian Bank): The number that keeps me
awake is 61% abandonment. When we interviewed people who dropped out, the two
words that came back were "slow" and "confusing".

PRIYA RAGHAVAN (Engagement Lead, Northgate Advisory): So what does good look
like?

AISHA BELL: The application must have fast response time so customers do not
abandon the journey. That is the headline.

PRIYA RAGHAVAN: Can we put a number on fast?

AISHA BELL: I would rather not commit to a number in the room. It needs to feel
instant. Engineering can tell us what is achievable.

AISHA BELL: The second one is comprehension. The interface should be easy to use
for customers who are not comfortable with digital banking. A meaningful share of
our applicants are over sixty.

MARCUS TRAN (Branch Operations Manager, Meridian Bank): We see that in branch
constantly. People give up at the income and employment section.

AISHA BELL: Right. So the system must explain in plain language why each piece of
information is being requested.

PRIYA RAGHAVAN: Accessibility standard?

AISHA BELL: This one I can be precise about because it is a bank policy. The
onboarding journey must conform to WCAG 2.2 Level AA. That is non-negotiable, it
is in our accessibility policy and we have been audited against it before.

AISHA BELL: And the journey must be available in English and Welsh.

PRIYA RAGHAVAN: Welsh because of the branch footprint?

AISHA BELL: Statutory. We have branches in Wales.

AISHA BELL: Last one from me. If the customer gets stuck, the system should offer
a handover to a human adviser without losing the data already entered.

MARCUS TRAN: That is a big one for us. Today the handover means starting again.

AISHA BELL: And it needs to be a good experience, not a jarring one. The design
should feel modern and reassuring.

PRIYA RAGHAVAN: I will capture that as a design principle rather than a
requirement, it is not testable as written.
